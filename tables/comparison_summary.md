# Cross-method comparison — Methods 1, 2 and 3

Generated by `analysis/comparison/cmp_01_compare.py` over the four frozen organ networks (538 distinct Key Events), in Ensembl identifier space.

**Where the gene sets come from.** Method 1 from the AOP-Wiki RDF at the pinned release. Methods 2 and 3 from the **molAOP Builder's own exports** (`/exports/gmt/ke-go` and `/exports/gmt/ke-wp`) — both were curated in the builder, so the comparison reports what the resource holds rather than an independent re-derivation. Both those exports carry HGNC symbols, so both methods incur the same symbol-to-Ensembl round-trip (99.4 % of 9620 symbols map; 56 do not). The builder serves a corpus snapshot of **WikiPathways 2026-07-10 · GO 2026-06-15**, which is behind the live resources — `data/comparison/source_route_drift.tsv` measures by how much.

Each method is limited by something different, and reading these as a ranking would attribute all three limits to the methods themselves:

- **Method 1** can only read a Key Event that HAS a free-text description.
- **Method 2** only covers Key Events a curator has annotated — 355 of the 538. The remaining 183 were never attempted.
- **Method 3** is limited by whether a molecular pathway exists for the Key Event at all, which is structural rather than a matter of effort.

## Do they agree on the genes themselves?

Only on Key Events where **both** methods produced a set. Jaccard and containment are both given because they answer different questions: the median annotated Key Event carries about 6 genes under Method 1 and about 65 under Methods 2 and 3, so a Method 1 set can sit entirely inside a Method 3 set and still score a low Jaccard. Containment reads: of the genes the first method found, what share the second also has.

| Pair | KEs | share >=1 gene | median Jaccard | A in B | B in A |
| --- | ---: | ---: | ---: | ---: | ---: |
| method1 vs method2 | 103 | 76.7 % | 0.015 | 22.2 % | 1.6 % |
| method1 vs method3 | 139 | 73.4 % | 0.032 | 27.3 % | 3.5 % |
| method2 vs method3 | 194 | 92.8 % | 0.055 | 16.9 % | 13.6 % |

At gene level over the four networks, the three methods name **9717 distinct genes** between them and agree on **609**: 201 are unique to Method 1, 5346 to Method 2 and 982 to Method 3.

The picture is one of **complementarity rather than redundancy**. Most Key Events annotated by two methods share at least one gene, but the median Key Event shares only a small minority of them, and each method contributes Key Events no other method reaches.
